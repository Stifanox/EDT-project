Marie Curie

Marie Curie, born Maria Sklodowska in Warsaw in 1867, was a physicist and chemist who conducted pioneering research on radioactivity. She moved to Paris in 1891 to study at the University of Paris.

Together with her husband Pierre Curie she discovered the elements polonium and radium in 1898. She shared the 1903 Nobel Prize in Physics with Pierre Curie and Henri Becquerel, and in 1911 she received the Nobel Prize in Chemistry, becoming the first person to win Nobel Prizes in two different sciences.

During the First World War she developed mobile X-ray units, known as "petites Curies", to help surgeons treat wounded soldiers. She died on 4 July 1934 from aplastic anaemia, most likely caused by long exposure to radiation. Her daughter Irène Joliot-Curie also won a Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1935.
