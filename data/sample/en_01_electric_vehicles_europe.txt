Electric Vehicles in Europe

Battery electric cars accounted for roughly one in seven new car registrations in the European Union in 2023, according to the European Automobile Manufacturers' Association (ACEA). Norway remains the global leader: the vast majority of new cars sold there are fully electric.

The European Parliament and the Council agreed that from 2035 new cars sold in the EU must have zero CO2 emissions, which effectively ends sales of new petrol and diesel cars. Manufacturers such as Volkswagen, Stellantis and Renault are investing billions of euros in battery factories, while Chinese brands like BYD are expanding quickly in Europe.

Charging infrastructure is still uneven. The Netherlands and Germany have dense public charging networks, while drivers in parts of Eastern and Southern Europe face long distances between fast chargers. Price, driving range and charging time remain the main concerns for buyers.
