The Paris Agreement

The Paris Agreement is an international treaty on climate change adopted on 12 December 2015 at the COP21 conference in Paris, organised under the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC). It entered into force on 4 November 2016.

Its central goal is to keep the increase in global average temperature well below 2 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels and to pursue efforts to limit it to 1.5 degrees. Each country submits a nationally determined contribution describing how it will reduce greenhouse gas emissions.

The United States withdrew from the agreement under President Donald Trump, a withdrawal that took effect in November 2020, and rejoined in February 2021 under President Joe Biden. Scientists from the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) warn that current national pledges are not sufficient to meet the 1.5 degree target.
